Read these numbers with care

  • Annual-review figures are shown as printed, with “over”, “more than” and “nearly” kept. Where a review does not state the period (traps, firearms), the label is “as reported in Annual Review 2025”.
  • Finance: the chart starts in 2022 because the group boundary changed that year. “Income” is the Statement of Financial Performance line. Total income including other and finance income is shown separately, with the sum.
  • The map shows Peace Parks' public ArcGIS layers as drawn. Status is exactly what the layer stores; blank or empty shows as “No status in layer”. No area totals are printed.

Four Mozambique parks, January 2020 to December 2024: Limpopo, Banhine, Zinave and Maputo National Parks. Satellite fire detections and rainfall, month by month, from open data.

A question, not a finding

Banhine's chart shows big dry-season peaks in most years, on the same scale as the other three parks. Planned burning, wildfire, or both? Field records would answer that.

Counts are satellite detections, not separate fires, and are not adjusted for park size. Hatched months may be undercounted. Read these numbers with care

  • Fire detections are not fires. Each one is a hot 375 m pixel seen by one NASA satellite at overpass. One fire can give many detections, and cloud or smoke can hide a fire.
  • Some months have missing satellite data. On some days there are no detections anywhere in Mozambique, which points to a satellite gap. Those months are hatched: Jul–Aug 2022, May–Jul 2024 and Nov 2024. Don't compare 2022 or 2024 yearly totals without that note.
  • Rainfall is a modelled satellite-plus-gauge estimate (CHIRPS), not a rain gauge in the park. The park value is the simple average of the 0.05° cells whose centre lies inside the park.
  • “Normal” means the 1991–2020 average for that park and month, from the same product. The difference is shown in mm.
  • Park outlines are Peace Parks' own published ArcGIS layer (SADC_Protected_Areas_Detailed_20250903), not gazetted legal boundaries. No park areas are printed. Maputo's outline is the land part only.
  • Month = UTC date. A few night detections on the 1st of a month fall in the previous month.
  • Includes 111 detections (2020–2024 files) FIRMS flags as type 3 (offshore/water), mostly in Banhine. All types kept. Files: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024.
  • Limpopo's 2020 counts are very low. The 2020 country file (FIRMS 2020) was checked complete. No explanation is offered. (No 2020 month is flagged for missing satellite data.)
  • Why the charts stop in December 2024: NASA's public yearly fire files end in 2024; 2025 onward needs a registered key. Rainfall uses the same window so the two line up.

How this was built and how every number was checked

Every number on pages 1 and 2 went through the same steps. Here they are, with the results.

The pipeline

  1. 1Public sourcesAnnual reviews 2019–2025, peaceparks.org posts, Peace Parks' ArcGIS layers, NASA FIRMS, CHIRPS.
  2. 2ExtractionEvery figure pulled with its exact sentence and PDF page.
  3. 3Independent re-derivationA second pass recomputed figures from the raw files without seeing the first.
  4. 4Sceptical reviewA sceptical reader with the reviews open hunted for anything to question.
  5. 5Clean tablesOne row per fact, with source, page, quote and access date on every row.
  6. 6This reportOnly cleared figures, each linked to its source.

How AI was used

AI was used to raise accuracy, not to generate numbers. An AI pass read every annual-review page and pulled each figure with its exact sentence and page. A second, independent pass recomputed every figure on these pages from the raw files without seeing the first. A third pass played a sceptical Peace Parks data manager and hunted for anything he would question. The AI is only as good as the data it is given, so every number here comes from a named public file and nothing is estimated.

What a single source of truth catches

Putting every published figure in one table, one row per fact, shows where two documents give different numbers for the same thing. Most have a simple reason: rounding, a different date, a new definition, or a real strategy update. Both values and both links are shown. A shared table makes these easy to spot and settle.

Why steady records matter

Peace Parks' own article on Maputo National Park reports that the number of reported elephant incidents has dropped.

“Either there are fewer incidents, or the intensity of reporting is dropping.”

Peace Parks' Head of Operations and Development in Maputo National Park, quoted in Building Futures: Maputo National Park's Strategy to Improve Human-Wildlife Co-existence, peaceparks.org, 31 July 2025 (accessed 2026-10-07).

Both readings fit the drop. A steady record of how incidents are reported (who reports, how, how often) would show which one it is. Months that were never recorded can't be filled in later.

Caveats, in plain words

  • Fire detections are not fires. Each one is a hot 375 m pixel seen by one NASA satellite at overpass. One fire can give many detections, and cloud or smoke can hide a fire.
  • Some months have missing satellite data. On some days there are no detections anywhere in Mozambique, which points to a satellite gap. Those months are hatched: Jul–Aug 2022, May–Jul 2024 and Nov 2024. Don't compare 2022 or 2024 yearly totals without that note.
  • Rainfall is a modelled satellite-plus-gauge estimate (CHIRPS), not a rain gauge in the park. The park value is the simple average of the 0.05° cells whose centre lies inside the park.
  • “Normal” means the 1991–2020 average for that park and month, from the same product. The difference is shown in mm.
  • Park outlines are Peace Parks' own published ArcGIS layer, not gazetted legal boundaries. No park areas are printed. Maputo's outline is the land part only.
  • Month = UTC date. A few night detections on the 1st of a month fall in the previous month.
  • Includes 111 detections (2020–2024 files) FIRMS flags as type 3 (offshore/water), mostly in Banhine. All types kept. Files: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024.
  • Annual-review figures are shown as printed, with “over”, “more than” and “nearly” kept. Where a review does not state the period (traps, firearms), the label is “as reported in Annual Review 2025”.
  • Finance: the trend line starts in 2022 because the group boundary changed that year. “Income” is the Statement of Financial Performance line. Total income including other and finance income is shown separately, with the sum.
  • Public data only. The report is not connected to any Peace Parks system, and it is a concept by Joseph Myers, not an official product.

What was deliberately not shown

  • No rhino, ranger, patrol or collar locations. No rhino moves at all, no fire points (monthly counts only), no patrol areas and no ranger numbers.
  • No personal data. ArcGIS editor usernames and editor notes were dropped. Officials' names were trimmed from quotes. No staff names.
  • No forecasts, projections or trend lines into the future. No “at this rate”. No % progress toward targets. No computed % changes. No annual moves derived by subtracting cumulative totals.
  • No modelled estimates presented as facts. The wildlife estimate and climate projections are not on pages 1–2.
